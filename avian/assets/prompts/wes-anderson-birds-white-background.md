# Wes Anderson-inspired Brisbane Bird Portraits — White Background

## Collection style guide

A coordinated library of 43 Brisbane birds rendered as precise, square, storybook editorial portraits. The visual language uses controlled symmetry, nostalgic colour, restrained theatrical styling and deadpan humour associated with *The Life Aquatic with Steve Zissou*, *The Grand Budapest Hotel* and *The French Dispatch*, without recreating film stills or actors. Each bird retains realistic species anatomy and receives exactly one bird-scaled character cue. Every image is isolated on flat pure white for later background removal.

## Eastern Spinebill

**Character inspiration:** French Dispatch junior reporter

**Required filenames**

- `acanthorhynchus-tenuirostris.png`
- `acanthorhynchus-tenuirostris-2.png`

### Shared art direction

Accurate Eastern Spinebill anatomy and natural field marks, with tiny press cap and restrained reporter styling. One restrained bird-scaled cue only; square centred storybook-editorial composition, deadpan expression, realistic plumage and a flat pure-white cutout background.

### Pose 1 prompt

Create one perfectly square, isolated full-body storybook editorial portrait for production as `acanthorhynchus-tenuirostris.png`.

SUBJECT — Eastern Spinebill (Acanthorhynchus tenuirostris), characterised as French Dispatch junior reporter. The bird must remain immediately and unmistakably identifiable as this species.

SPECIES LOCK — Preserve these non-negotiable natural field marks: a very long fine downward-curved bill; black crown and face; white throat with a chestnut throat patch; buff underparts; compact honeyeater proportions. Maintain realistic avian anatomy and proportions, including the correct beak, eyes, wings, tail, legs, feet and toe arrangement. Do not substitute a similar species or create human anatomy.

VISUAL LANGUAGE — Use precise centred composition, controlled symmetry, nostalgic mid-century European colour sensibility, meticulous theatrical art direction, quiet storybook humour and a mildly solemn deadpan mood. Draw inspiration from refined hotel formality, maritime expedition styling and illustrated magazine portraiture. Use subtle photographic or painterly texture on the bird only. Do not recreate a film still, actor or existing frame.

SINGLE CHARACTER CUE — Use only this restrained cue: tiny press cap and restrained reporter styling. It must be one small, bird-scaled accessory or styling treatment that supports species recognition. Any hat, cap, spectacles, tie or uniform reference must be physically fitted to an ordinary bird body: no sleeves, arms, hands, human torso, trousers or shoes. No second accessory, prop, insignia, lettering, brand mark or decorative surface pattern.

POSE — Show the bird front-facing or within a few degrees of front-facing, standing naturally with both feet and every toe visible. Build a strongly centred, near-bilateral hero composition and a quiet, deadpan, faintly perplexed expression.

FRAMING — Use a perfectly square 1:1 canvas. Centre one bird only and show the complete silhouette, including crest, beak, folded wings, entire tail, both legs, both feet and every toe. Keep the bird at roughly 65 to 75 percent of the canvas height and width, with a broad, clearly visible band of white beyond every extremity. No cropping and nothing may touch an image edge.

BACKGROUND — Plain pure white background, RGB 255, 255, 255. No off-white, cream, grey or tint. No shadow, cast shadow or contact shadow. No floor line, horizon, texture, paper grain, canvas grain, gradient, glow, halo, reflection, coloured spill, scenery, architecture, furniture, branch, perch, support, text, caption, logo, watermark, border or frame. Preserve a clean cutout-friendly silhouette with crisp fine feather edges.
